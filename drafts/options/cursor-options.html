Landscape hover cue

Clicking the home landscape starts a new descent, but nothing on screen says so. Each strip below is the real home surface with one way of signalling that the map can be clicked. Hover, then click in each one.

Phones have no hover, so every option shares one touch fallback: on first view, a single ring pulses where the opening descent starts and the readout says “Tap the map to start another descent”. It shows once and disappears after the first tap.

A

Start marker

Over the map the pointer becomes the solver’s own start ring, labelled x₀ with “click to descend” beside it. The label shortens to x₀ after the first descent.

Says“Put a start point here.”
Reduced motionUnchanged: the ring follows the pointer and nothing animates on its own.
CostSmallest. A custom cursor and one label.
B · recommended

Ghost descent

While you hover, the path the solver would take from the pointer is drawn faintly to the minimum it would reach, and that project’s label is outlined. Clicking runs that same path for real, with the iteration readout.

Says“Click and this is what happens.” It also shows which project you will land on.
Reduced motionThe preview still follows the pointer, which is direct manipulation. A click draws the finished path at once.
CostRuns the solver once per frame while hovering, about 200 gradient steps. Cheap on any laptop.
C

Gradient needle

A short arrow at the pointer shows −∇f, the direction a descent would head first. It lengthens on steep slopes and shrinks to a dot at the bottom of a basin.

Says“This is a surface things roll down.” The most mathematical of the four.
Reduced motionUnchanged: the needle only moves with the pointer.
CostSmall. It reads as a surface, but says less about clicking than A or B.
D

Ripple invitation

When the pointer rests on the map, a ring spreads from it every second and a half, like a drop on water, with a “Click to start a descent” pill. The pill retires after the first click.

Says“Click here,” in the most familiar way.
Reduced motionNo ripple. The pill appears on rest.
CostSmall, but the most generic and the least tied to the solver.
Pick

Recommendation

B, with A’s ring as the pointer. The cue is drawn by the same solver the click runs, so the hover shows exactly what will happen and where you will land. That follows the site’s rule that every visual comes from the work’s own mathematics. A alone is the fallback if B feels busy over the name and links.

Scroll tilt

Your side note, as a working prototype. The same surface is drawn as a height field, and scrolling through this section tips it from the flat contour map into 3D. Minima become pits and the descent path lies on the terrain. Click the map to run a descent at any angle.

Pitch 0°Scroll to tilt

How it is built. The tilt is a canvas projection of the same function the solver uses, painted back to front, with no WebGL and no library. Scroll position maps straight to pitch. anime.js v4’s onScroll observer could drive the same value with easing and snap points. It would be the site’s first dependency, so I’d add it only if we want those extras.

Where it would go. The home hero, as you scroll from the landscape into the Latest strip. The tilt ends before the strip takes over, and scrolling back up flattens it again. Under reduced motion the map stays flat.

Open question. At a steep pitch the project labels lose their alignment with the minima they name. The prototype keeps them pinned to the projected pits. If that reads poorly on the real hero, the labels can fade out above about 40°.
